## Abstract

Institutional maintenance management becomes increasingly challenging as organizations expand their physical infrastructure. Traditional paper-based and manual processes can lead to inefficient request handling, delayed repairs, fragmented communication, and increased operational costs.

This paper examines digital maintenance-tracking platforms for environments such as universities, hospitals, and corporate campuses. It discusses how cloud computing, workflow automation, data analytics, mobile accessibility, real-time notifications, and predictive-maintenance capabilities can support the maintenance process—from submitting a request through task completion and feedback.

The study includes an analysis of existing maintenance-management approaches, system architecture considerations, and an implementation methodology. It also discusses adoption, integration, scalability, user experience, and organizational change as important factors in successful deployment.

## Research focus

- **Facility and asset management:** organizing maintenance activities and institutional infrastructure.
- **Maintenance request workflows:** tracking requests from submission to resolution.
- **Workflow automation:** reducing manual coordination and improving process consistency.
- **Cloud computing:** supporting scalable and accessible maintenance platforms.
- **Mobile accessibility and notifications:** helping users and maintenance teams access timely information.
- **Data analytics and predictive maintenance:** exploring ways to identify patterns and anticipate equipment issues.
- **User adoption and usability:** considering the needs of requesters, technicians, supervisors, and administrators.
- **System integration and scalability:** considering how a platform can fit into a larger institutional technology environment.

## Problem addressed

Manual maintenance processes often distribute requests across phone calls, emails, paper forms, and informal conversations. This can make requests difficult to track, create communication gaps, reduce visibility into work progress, and make reporting or accountability harder.

The paper examines how a centralized digital maintenance-tracking approach can address these process challenges.

## Research objectives

The research aims to:

1. Examine existing institutional maintenance-management approaches and identify their limitations.
2. Study an architecture for a digital maintenance-tracking platform.
3. Consider the requirements of different institutional user groups.
4. Explore workflow automation, mobile access, cloud infrastructure, and integration capabilities.
5. Discuss implementation, user adoption, and continuous-improvement considerations.
